\begin{abstract}
Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is widely proposed for O-RAN slice
admission control, but most of this work -- including our
own~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2} -- is validated only in simulation,
a gap our systematic review of 67 core 2024--2026 studies formalized as
the field's \emph{validated-deployment gap}~\cite{survey-paper3}. This
paper narrows that gap -- realizing the control loop on real
protocol-stack, MAC-scheduler, and E2-agent hardware, radio channel still
simulated (rfsim) -- and centers the evaluation on quality of experience
(QoE): a QoE-aware DQN admission policy
($r=\alpha\cdot\text{MOS}-\beta\cdot\text{cost}-\xi\cdot\text{SLA\_viol}$)
deployed on a real OpenAirInterface (OAI) gNB across three slices, against
a static baseline, its SLA-only counterpart, and a static-at-cap oracle
that removes the admission decision itself (every ceiling parked at its
calibrated maximum, never adapted). Live, DQN holds $100.0\pm0.0\%$ SLA
compliance against the baseline's seed-dependent $73.6\pm18.6\%$ (worst
episode $0.0\%$) across 45 episodes, and -- unlike the oracle, which still
collapses into the same catastrophic-backlog regime in 4 of 15 episodes
($p=0.0149$, DQN 25/25 vs.\ oracle 11/15 fully compliant over a
reverification round) -- this rules out ``DQN just rediscovers a fixed
high ceiling'' as an explanation for the result. We disclose the
engineering numbers a real deployment needs (sub-millisecond E2 round
trip, sub-100-$\mu$s inference, a control loop holding its configured
5\,s cadence to within 1\,ms), and report two independent MOS-calibration
bugs found and fixed mid-study (a throughput units mismatch; QoE scored
against generic rather than each slice's configured SLA deadline), with a
directional, not yet fully-powered, live re-run under the correction. A
held-out, offline-only experiment under genuinely congested, dynamic,
randomized multi-slice traffic with real shared-PRB contention shows DQN,
once trained with a correctly decaying exploration schedule, preserves
URLLC SLA compliance under either reward ($22.6\%\rightarrow30.9\%$
SLA-only, $\rightarrow27.0\%$ QoE-aware) by trading off eMBB
($\rightarrow7.9\%$/$8.2\%$) and mMTC ($\rightarrow9.2\%$/$10.8\%$) -- the
URLLC-first outcome our prior simulated work targeted, now shown under
real cross-slice scarcity and a property of the trained policy rather
than the specific reward formulation, though not yet a win on this
paper's own priority-weighted utility metric. We report where
offline-to-live transfer does and does not hold, and position this
evidence against the five validation directions our review's proposed
QoE-aware GAT-MARL-FL framework calls out.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
O-RAN, network slicing, deep reinforcement learning, admission control, quality of experience, URLLC prioritization, live testbed
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Network slicing lets one physical O-RAN deployment serve heterogeneous
service classes (eMBB, URLLC, mMTC) under per-slice SLA guarantees, but
static, threshold-based control cannot adapt PRB allocation as demand
shifts~\cite{slicing-survey}. DRL is a natural fit: our own prior work
formulated single-gNB SLA-aware slice admission control~\cite{mecon-paper1}
and extended it to a third slice with a multi-gNB load-balancing
term~\cite{icraie-paper2}, reporting $\approx$99.5\% and
99.63/88.41/97.64\% per-slice URLLC/eMBB/mMTC SLA satisfaction
respectively -- both validated entirely in simulation. Our systematic
review of the 2024--2026 literature~\cite{survey-paper3} (6{,}286 records
screened to 67 core studies) found this pattern holds across the field
and crystallized it as one of five recurring gaps -- alongside QoE
inference, topology awareness, privacy preservation, and scalability --
motivating a proposed QoE-aware GAT-MARL-FL framework
(graph-attention state encoding~\cite{gat}, centralized-training
multi-agent control, and federated learning~\cite{fedavg} across gNBs)
to close them.

This paper makes five contributions. \textbf{(1)} We realize the slice
admission-control MDP from~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2} on a real
OAI gNB over its native, RIC-free E2 control loop -- not simulation --
directly addressing the validated-deployment gap, and surface a
structural finding for anyone attempting this on real O-RAN hardware:
the real E2 agent exposes no per-flow accept/reject primitive, only a
per-slice PRB ratio ceiling, so admission must be realized as
\emph{ceiling nudging}. \textbf{(2)} We center the evaluation on QoE,
comparing a QoE-aware reward directly against the SLA-only reward
of~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2} on the same live hardware.
\textbf{(3)} We report offline-simulated and live results side by side
for the same frozen policies, showing where simulation-to-real transfer
holds and where it does not. \textbf{(4)} We add a config-only
\emph{static-at-cap} oracle arm -- every ceiling parked at its calibrated
maximum, never adapted -- to isolate genuine admission timing from just
riding every ceiling upward, and disclose the engineering numbers and two
QoE/MOS calibration bugs a real deployment surfaces that offline work
does not. \textbf{(5)} We build a genuinely congested, dynamic,
randomized multi-slice scenario with real shared-PRB contention and show
a properly-trained DQN policy, under either reward, learns to preserve
URLLC at the deliberate expense of eMBB/mMTC -- the same URLLC-first
objective as~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2}, now under real
cross-slice scarcity rather than isolated per-slice utility.

\emph{Scope.} Single gNB, three UEs (one per slice), rfsim. The three
slices already contend for one shared 106-PRB pool -- a genuine,
small-scale multi-agent coordination problem. An explicit multi-agent
(GAT-MARL) controller over that pool, multi-gNB coordination, and
federated learning -- our review's proposed framework in
full~\cite{survey-paper3} -- are the journal-length follow-up's target
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), not attempted here.

\section{System Architecture and Testbed}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the control loop: an Open5GS core with
three slice-specific SMF/UPF pairs, an OAI gNB (ORANSlice
fork~\cite{oranslice}, RIC-free UDP E2 agent built into the gNB binary,
distinct from vanilla OAI's FlexRIC~\cite{oai,oran-architecture}), and
three \texttt{nr-uesoftmodem} UEs, one per slice. The E2 agent's only
control primitive is \texttt{slicing\_control\_m}\{sst, sd, min\_ratio,
max\_ratio\}~\cite{oran-e2ap,oran-e2sm-kpm} -- a per-slice PRB ratio
ceiling enforced by the MAC scheduler, with no raw per-flow accept/reject
hook. The binary admission decision of~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2}
is therefore realized as ceiling nudging: accept raises a slice's
\texttt{max\_ratio} toward a calibrated cap, reject lowers it toward its
floor. Ratio caps were calibrated by direct live measurement, not assumed
linear (Table~\ref{tab:params}): the 0--100 ratio scale does not map
linearly onto real PRBs at this cell's 106-PRB/$\mu{=}1$ configuration,
so each slice's cap was set empirically below its measured organic
demand. The static baseline pins each ceiling-step size to zero (no
accept/reject can move it all episode); learned arms are DQN~\cite{dqn}
policies trained offline under an SLA-only (Eq.~\ref{eq:sla}) or
QoE-aware reward (Eq.~\ref{eq:qoe}) against a closed-loop synthetic KPM
source, then evaluated live with frozen weights.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}
Each admission decision at step $t$ accepts or rejects incoming demand
for slice $s\in\mathcal{S}=\{\text{eMBB},\text{URLLC},\text{mMTC}\}$,
realized as the ceiling adjustment above. Two reward formulations are
compared. The SLA-only reward (eq.~2 of~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2}):
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\text{SLA}} = \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} w_s c_s n_{s,t}^{\text{acc}}
  \;-\; \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \mathbb{1}[v_{s,t}]\, p_s
  \;-\; \eta\, \bar\rho_t\, N_t^{\text{acc}}
\label{eq:sla}
\end{equation}
where $w_s$/$p_s$ are slice $s$'s priority weight and violation penalty
(URLLC highest of the three, Table~\ref{tab:params}), $n_{s,t}^{\text{acc}}$
is slice $s$'s accepted count, $\mathbb{1}[v_{s,t}]$ flags an SLA
violation, and $\eta\bar\rho_t N_t^{\text{acc}}$ penalizes total accepted
volume by mean cluster congestion $\bar\rho_t$. The QoE-aware reward
(eq.~3 of~\cite{survey-paper3}), used to center this paper's evaluation
on QoE rather than SLA alone:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\text{QoE}} = \alpha\, \widehat{\text{MOS}}_t \;-\; \beta\, \bar\rho_t N_t^{\text{acc}} \;-\; \xi\, \overline{\text{SLA\_viol}}_t
\label{eq:qoe}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha{=}1.0,\beta{=}0.2,\xi{=}0.5$ (matching~\cite{survey-paper3}'s
own symbol for the SLA-violation weight) and
$\widehat{\text{MOS}}_t\in[0,1]$ the mean inferred per-slice MOS,
normalized from its native P.1203/ACR 1--5 scale via
$(\text{MOS}-1)/4$ (Table~\ref{tab:results} reports the raw,
un-normalized 1--5 values). MOS itself is estimated with a
Weber--Fechner-law closed-form prior -- eq.~4 of~\cite{survey-paper3},
$\text{MOS}=a-b\ln(1+Q_{\text{deg}})$, distinct from the exponential
IQX-hypothesis QoE-mapping family~\cite{iqx-hypothesis} -- refined by a
per-slice LSTM, calibrated against ITU-T P.1203~\cite{itu-p1203}
objective labels for eMBB and ACR-style task-success scoring for
URLLC/mMTC, and $\overline{\text{SLA\_viol}}_t\in[0,1]$ the mean
per-slice violation \emph{severity} (continuous margin, not a flat
per-violation charge).
Section~\ref{sec:congested}'s congested scenario additionally enforces a
genuine shared-PRB-pool constraint absent from the base offline
simulator: each slice's requested PRBs $\text{req}_{s,t}$ are served in
full only while the pool has slack, otherwise every slice is rationed
proportionally to its own request share,
\begin{equation}
\text{served}_{s,t} =
\begin{cases}
\text{req}_{s,t}, & \sum_{s'}\text{req}_{s',t} \le B \\[2pt]
\text{req}_{s,t}\cdot \dfrac{B}{\sum_{s'}\text{req}_{s',t}}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
with $B$ the shared PRB budget. This rationing rule is priority-neutral
by construction: any URLLC protection observed downstream must come from
the policy's reward-driven behavior, not from the physics.

Raw per-slice compliance treats every slice as equally important, which
Table~\ref{tab:params}'s own $w_s$ (priority weight) says is not the
case. We therefore also report a priority-weighted SLA utility,
\begin{equation}
U = \frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} w_s \cdot \text{compliance}_s}{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} w_s},
\label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
using each experiment's own declared $w_s$ (Table~\ref{tab:params}),
alongside the raw per-slice figures, not instead of them -- $U$ answers
"how well does this arm do against the objective the reward actually
declares," which an unweighted mean across slices of unequal declared
importance does not.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Live Testbed: DQN vs.\ Static Baseline}
\label{sec:live}
Fig.~\ref{fig:ceiling} shows the mechanism directly: baseline's
commanded ceiling is bit-for-bit flat at its static nominal ratio for
the entire episode, while DQN rides eMBB's ceiling from floor to its
calibrated cap within $\sim$13 steps and jumps URLLC/mMTC to their caps
immediately, holding both for the remainder. The direct consequence,
across 45 live episodes (3 seeds $\times$ 5 episodes $\times$ 2 reward
modes) on real hardware: DQN under both the SLA-only and QoE-aware
reward holds $100.0\pm0.0\%$ SLA compliance on every slice, in every
episode, against the static baseline's $73.6\pm18.6\%$ (bootstrap 95\%
CI $[53.4,93.4]$), with a worst single episode of $0.0\%$ for baseline
versus $100.0\%$ for DQN (Fig.~\ref{fig:compliance},
Table~\ref{tab:results}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/fig4_ceiling_trajectories.pdf}
\caption{Commanded PRB ceiling per slice over one representative episode: baseline (flat) vs.\ DQN (rides to the calibrated cap, annotated per slice).}
\label{fig:ceiling}
\end{figure} Baseline's compliance is real but
\emph{seed-dependent} (60.3\%/100.0\%/60.7\% across the three seeds) --
the practical value demonstrated is reliability under variable
conditions, not rescuing an otherwise-always-failing baseline. We do not
report Cohen's $d$: every DQN arm's per-seed compliance has zero
variance, so a pooled-variance effect size would be driven entirely by
baseline's own spread. We omit per-slice inferred MOS from
Table~\ref{tab:results}: it was computed under a calibration later found,
diagnosed, and fixed to carry a units bug specifically affecting
eMBB/URLLC (Section~\ref{sec:recal} reports the corrected picture and why
the SLA-compliance numbers here are unaffected by it).

A natural objection: does DQN's advantage reduce to simply parking every
ceiling at a generous fixed value, something a differently-configured
static policy could match without learning anything? We add
\texttt{static-at-cap}, a config-only fourth arm that pins every ceiling
at its calibrated cap from step zero (baseline's own
\texttt{AlwaysAcceptPolicy} entrypoint, unmodified) -- the state DQN
itself converges to and holds by episode end (Fig.~\ref{fig:ceiling}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:compliance} and
Table~\ref{tab:results} show it is not equivalent to DQN: static-at-cap
collapses into the same catastrophic backlog-blowup regime baseline
exhibits in 4 of its 15 episodes (27\%, statistically indistinguishable
from baseline's own 4/15 -- Fisher exact $p=1.0$), while DQN,
re-evaluated across a reverification round on 5 distinct seeds (2 never
used in the original campaign) for 25 episodes total, never collapsed
once (static-at-cap 11/15 vs.\ DQN 25/25 fully compliant, Fisher exact
$p=0.0149$). In episodes where it does not collapse, static-at-cap's
severity distribution is statistically indistinguishable from DQN's --
\textbf{the mechanism is the admission decision, not the ceiling value}:
neither baseline nor static-at-cap ever rejects under backlog pressure;
DQN's contribution is learning \emph{when} to reject, which no fixed
ceiling, however generously set, can do.

\subsection{Engineering Instrumentation: Is This Deployable?}
\label{sec:engineering}
Measured directly against the real gNB E2 agent and the real, frozen
checkpoints on CPU (no GPU on this near-RT path): E2 \texttt{poll()}
round trip 0.566\,ms median (1.773\,ms p99); \texttt{send\_control()}
0.168\,ms (send-call overhead only -- this E2 agent returns no response
to CONTROL messages, fire-and-forget); DQN inference 68\,$\mu$s median
(115\,$\mu$s p99) on a 307\,KB, 18,562-parameter checkpoint; control-loop
cadence 5.0007\,s measured against a configured 5.000\,s -- a naive
estimate from batched subprocess wall-clock time had instead suggested a
spurious $\approx$85--90\,ms/step gap, traced to a fixed
$\approx$8--9\,s per-launch startup cost amortized over a batch's episode
count, not a per-step cost, and absent from every compliance/utility
number in this paper. The per-step cost that does dominate is QoE
inference, not RL decision-making: the MOS-mapper LSTM alone costs
$\approx$7.3\,ms against the DQN network's $\approx$0.07\,ms and the E2
protocol's $\approx$0.6\,ms -- all under 2\% of budget.

\subsection{Offline vs.\ Online: Sim-to-Real Transfer}
\label{sec:sim2real}
DQN is trained offline against a closed-loop synthetic KPM source, then
evaluated live with frozen weights; Fig.~\ref{fig:sim2real} compares each
arm's offline-predicted compliance (final 5 rollup episodes/seed, pooled
across 3 training seeds) against its live-measured compliance. The
result is not a clean diagonal. eMBB transfers exactly:
$100.0\%\rightarrow100.0\%$ for both reward modes. URLLC and mMTC do
not: offline-predicted compliance is exactly $0.0\%$ for both slices,
both arms, throughout all 300 training episodes, while the same frozen
policies measure $100.0\%$ live -- not an accidental scale mismatch: the
offline training config deliberately pins URLLC/mMTC's ceiling at nominal
ratio with zero elastic headroom, an oversubscription-by-construction
regime that never intended offline compliance to predict live behavior
for these two slices. The SLA-only reward therefore had no
compliance-based learning signal to differentiate policies on
URLLC/mMTC offline -- whatever separates live URLLC/mMTC behavior across
arms was not something the offline reward could have selected for. This
ceiling-headroom mismatch is distinct from, and not resolved by,
Section~\ref{sec:recal}'s later demand-scale fix; we flag it as open
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), not as a result to spin.

\subsection{QoE/MOS Recalibration: Two Bugs Found and Fixed}
\label{sec:recal}
Section~\ref{sec:live}'s omitted MOS column reflects two calibration bugs
found and fixed, not floor-pinned QoE. \textbf{Bug 1:} the frozen
environment feeds \texttt{prb\_used\_ratio} (usage as a fraction of the
gNB's 100-PRB capacity) into the MOS mapper's throughput input, but the
frozen calibration script fit that input as an absolute PRB/UE count in
$[0.05,5.0]$ -- eMBB's real $\approx$15-PRB demand (ratio$\approx$0.15)
read as almost-starved, not well-served near its 12-PRB ceiling.
\textbf{Bug 2:} URLLC/mMTC's MOS labels were scored against generic
default SLA deadlines (5\,ms/0.1\%, 1000\,ms/5\%), not this campaign's
real thresholds (20\,ms/0.5\%, 65\,ms/3.5\%, Table~\ref{tab:params}).
Both fixed and verified at real operating points: eMBB MOS at ratio 0.15
moves $1.000\rightarrow3.624$ ($r{=}0.987$); URLLC MOS at its real
20\,ms deadline moves $1.000\rightarrow3.525$. Neither bug touches the
SLA-compliance/margin path Section~\ref{sec:live}'s numbers come from --
only the omitted MOS reading needed correction.

A third, related bug surfaced while retraining under the fix: the
offline environment's demand-scale formula produces $\approx$4$\times$
less mean load than real live traffic, leaving the corrected calibration
nothing to differentiate. We fixed this with a new environment factory
driven by real measured demand ratios, validated zero-training before
spending compute, and retrained both DQN checkpoints under all three
fixes. A directional live check (3 seeds$\times$2 episodes, cut by
explicit time budget) reproduces Section~\ref{sec:live}'s qualitative
picture (baseline $U{=}67.0\%$ vs.\ 100.0\% for both DQN arms), MOS no
longer floor-pinned (eMBB 1.91--2.17 vs.\ $\approx$1.2 before). A further
round asked whether static-at-cap's collapse-avoidance finding survives
the correction: it replicates directionally (DQN 16/16 vs.\
static-at-cap 19/21) but not at significance ($p{=}0.495$ vs.\
$p{=}0.0149$ above) -- reported as such, not rounded up. A fully-powered
live re-run under this correction is this paper's most concrete next
experiment (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Congested, URLLC-Prioritized Admission}
\label{sec:congested}
Sections~\ref{sec:live}--\ref{sec:sim2real} use one constant per-slice
demand level per episode. This section asks a sharper, QoE-motivated
question offline: under genuinely congested, dynamic, randomized
multi-slice traffic with real shared-PRB contention (Eq.~\ref{eq:pool}),
does DQN preserve URLLC's SLA compliance ahead of eMBB/mMTC, consistent
with URLLC already carrying the highest reward priority
(Table~\ref{tab:params})? This required two fixes to the base offline
simulator: matching its ceiling headroom to the live config
(Section~\ref{sec:sim2real}'s finding), and adding the shared-pool
constraint of Eq.~\ref{eq:pool} (calibrated to $B{=}8$, smaller than the
three ceilings' combined maximum of 19 PRB, so maxing every ceiling
simultaneously creates genuine scarcity), since the base simulator
computed each slice's served PRBs independently and never let one
slice's ceiling take capacity from another. Per-episode demand is
additionally randomized ($0.4\times$--$1.3\times$ each slice's mean) and
remains dynamic within-episode.

Table~\ref{tab:congested} and Fig.~\ref{fig:congested} show the held-out
result, trained with a corrected exploration schedule: an earlier version
of this experiment left \texttt{DQNAdmissionPolicy}'s per-episode epsilon
decay uncalled, so training-time action selection was uniform-random
throughout (confirmed via checkpoint inspection, $\epsilon=1.0$ after
$\sim$18{,}000 gradient steps) rather than the intended decay from full
exploration to near-greedy; Q-learning is off-policy and still learns
from the randomly-collected, reward-labeled transitions, but this is not
the schedule Table~\ref{tab:params} intends, so we fixed it and retrained
both arms before reporting final numbers.\footnote{A2C~\cite{a2c}, also
trained under this protocol ($\epsilon$-schedule bug not applicable),
converged to a fully deterministic, state-independent policy regardless
of exploration/entropy interventions -- an algorithm-dependent
limitation, omitted from the headline comparison.} With the fix, both DQN arms
improve URLLC above baseline ($22.6\%\rightarrow30.9\%$ SLA-only,
$\rightarrow27.0\%$ QoE-aware) by actively \emph{sacrificing} eMBB
($34.5\%\rightarrow7.9\%$/$8.2\%$) and mMTC
($19.0\%\rightarrow9.2\%$/$10.8\%$) -- a genuine, learned priority
tradeoff, not a policy that merely rides every ceiling upward. This is no
longer reward-mode-specific: an earlier, since-corrected run had shown
only the QoE-shaped reward producing this tradeoff; with exploration
correctly decaying, \texttt{DQN(SLA)} shows the same tradeoff as
\texttt{DQN(QoE)}, if anything a marginally larger URLLC gain --
evidence the tradeoff is a property of a properly-trained DQN policy
under this priority structure (Table~\ref{tab:params}), not a
QoE-reward-specific effect, a more robust but less differentiated finding
than we initially reported. Measured against the priority-weighted
utility $U$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:utility}), \emph{baseline still leads}
($U{=}27.2\%$ vs.\ $21.0\%$/$19.0\%$, Table~\ref{tab:congested}): eMBB's
declared priority weight (3.5) is large enough that the compliance it
gives up outweighs URLLC's gain under Table~\ref{tab:params}'s actual
weights. The tradeoff is real and reproducible, but not yet a utility
improvement against this paper's own declared objective -- whether to
revisit the tradeoff or the priority weights that make it
utility-negative is open for the journal-length follow-up. This offline,
preliminary result is not yet live-validated (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What This Paper Achieves Relative to Our Review}
Our review~\cite{survey-paper3} identified five recurring gaps --
validated deployment, QoE inference, topology awareness, privacy
preservation, and scalability -- and proposed a QoE-aware GAT-MARL-FL
framework conditioned on five validation directions. This paper
\textbf{narrows} validated deployment for slice admission control:
protocol stack, MAC scheduler, E2 loop, and core network real; radio
channel still rfsim-simulated. Sections~\ref{sec:live}--\ref{sec:sim2real}
show DRL-based admission, realized as ceiling nudging on real E2
hardware, reliably beats both a static ratio and a static-at-cap oracle
under constant load, with the deployment engineering numbers already
measured (Section~\ref{sec:engineering}). It contributes \textbf{partial}
evidence toward QoE inference (Section~\ref{sec:recal} found and fixed
two calibration bugs; corrected MOS is no longer floor-pinned, but a
fully-powered live re-run remains outstanding) and toward
topology/scalability (Section~\ref{sec:congested} is a first offline
demonstration that shared-resource contention is learnable by a
single-agent controller, not yet across multiple gNBs or with a
topology-aware state). Privacy preservation (FL) remains
\textbf{untouched}: single-rig, single-tenant by construction.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Results are from OAI's rfsim radio simulator, not over-the-air
deployment. The testbed runs on a single shared desktop machine, subject
to intermittent radio-link failure under memory pressure, mitigated by
automatic health-check-and-restart (verified to recover every time);
every evaluation episode counted here completed and was log-verified end
to end. A single gNB and three UEs were used, so paper \#2's multi-gNB
load-balancing term is not evaluated here. Live evaluation used 3 seeds
per arm. Per-slice MOS is an inferred quantity, not a measurement from
real end-user subjective scoring, and Section~\ref{sec:live}'s headline
table omits it rather than report the pre-correction value
(Section~\ref{sec:recal}). Section~\ref{sec:recal}'s corrected-calibration
live numbers are directional only ($n{=}6$--21/arm, smaller than
Section~\ref{sec:live}'s fully-powered $n{=}15$/arm), so the static-at-cap
collapse-avoidance finding is direction-replicated but not re-confirmed
under the correction ($p{=}0.495$ vs.\ $p{=}0.0149$), not settled twice.
Genuine live demand-driven scarcity for Section~\ref{sec:congested} was
assessed, not attempted, for the resource reasons in Table~\ref{tab:gaps}.
Section~\ref{sec:congested}'s result is offline-only, evaluates DQN only
(A2C's contrasting, degenerate behavior is reported in a footnote there
but not part of this paper's live-validated results), and has not been
reproduced on the live rig.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper realizes the slice admission-control MDP
from~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2} on a real OAI gNB over its native
RIC-free E2 loop -- protocol stack, MAC scheduler, core network real,
radio channel still rfsim-simulated -- narrowing our own systematic
review's~\cite{survey-paper3} validated-deployment gap, and centers
evaluation on QoE rather than SLA compliance alone. Across 45 live episodes on real hardware, DQN holds
$100.0\pm0.0\%$ SLA compliance under both SLA-only and QoE-aware rewards
against a static baseline's seed-dependent $73.6\pm18.6\%$ (worst episode
$0.0\%$); a static-at-cap oracle that removes the admission decision
itself collapses at baseline's own rate (4/15 episodes, statistically
tied), while DQN never collapsed once across 25 reverified episodes
($p{=}0.0149$) -- the mechanism is the admission decision, not the
ceiling value. We disclose the engineering numbers a deployment needs
(sub-ms E2 round trip, sub-100-$\mu$s inference, 5\,s cadence held to
within 1\,ms) and fix two MOS-calibration bugs found mid-study; a
directional live re-run under the correction reproduces this picture but
does not yet re-confirm the oracle-collapse finding at full power.
Comparing offline-simulated and live results side by side shows
transfer holds exactly for eMBB but not for URLLC/mMTC, a diagnosed,
fixable limitation of the offline training environment rather than a
robustness result. A preliminary, offline-only follow-up under genuine
cross-slice PRB contention shows a properly-trained DQN policy learns to
preserve URLLC SLA compliance under either reward
($22.6\%\rightarrow30.9\%$ SLA-only, $\rightarrow27.0\%$ QoE-aware) by
actively trading off eMBB ($\rightarrow7.9\%$/$8.2\%$) and mMTC
($\rightarrow9.2\%$/$10.8\%$) -- the same URLLC-first objective as our
prior simulated work~\cite{mecon-paper1,icraie-paper2}, now shown under
real contention and, once a training-time exploration-schedule bug we
fixed was corrected, a property of the trained policy rather than of the
QoE-shaped reward, though not yet a win on this paper's own
priority-weighted utility. The proposed GAT-MARL-FL
framework~\cite{survey-paper3} remains the journal-length follow-up's
target, motivated by this paper's finding that single-agent control needs
careful, manual reward shaping to achieve the URLLC-priority tradeoff.
